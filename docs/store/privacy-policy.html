Peril Point privacy policy

Effective October 14, 2026.

Peril Point is an arcade game where you steer a small ship through a swarm. It is published by NewZenith Datacom OPC, a company in the Philippines (“we”). This policy explains what the game keeps on your phone, what the ads in it collect, and the choices you have.

In short

  • There are no accounts. Your progress and settings stay on your phone, and we never receive a copy.
  • The game shows ads from Google AdMob. To show them, Google’s ad software in the game collects some information about your device, such as its advertising ID and IP address, and sends it to Google.
  • In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, the game asks for your consent before showing personalised ads. You can change your choice at any time in the game’s Settings.

What Peril Point keeps on your phone

  • Your game progress: points, upgrades, ships, arena themes and skins, goals, achievements and best scores.
  • Your settings: steering, handling, stick position, tilt level and calibration, audio, vibration and your data choices.
  • Ad timing: how many games you have played and how long you have played since the last ad, so ads aren’t shown too often.

This stays in the game’s private storage on your phone. It isn’t included in Android backups, and it isn’t sent to us or to anyone else. Peril Point has no servers of its own.

What Peril Point uses

  • Touch and motion sensors. You can steer by dragging or by tilting the phone. While the game is open, it reads the motion sensor to steer your ship and to calibrate tilt. It doesn’t record, store or send those readings, apart from the one calibration value it keeps on your phone so tilt steering feels level.
  • Vibration, for in-game effects. You can turn it off in Settings.
  • Internet and network state, used only to load and show ads.
  • Advertising ID and Android ad services. Google’s ad software adds these permissions so it can use your device’s advertising ID and Android’s privacy-preserving ad features.

Google’s ad software also adds two technical permissions it uses to do its work: keeping the device awake and running a foreground service. The game doesn’t use your location, camera, microphone, contacts, photos or files.

Ads

Peril Point is free and is paid for by ads from Google AdMob:

  • A full-screen ad between runs, at most once every few games or few minutes of play, when you tap Retry or Menu after a run.
  • An optional ad you can choose to watch after a run (Watch ad · 3× points) to triple the points you earned. It plays only if you tap it.

To show and measure ads, and to prevent fraud and abuse, Google’s Mobile Ads SDK collects and sends to Google:

  • device identifiers: your device’s advertising ID and app set ID;
  • your IP address, from which an approximate location can be inferred;
  • information about your device and the app, such as the phone model, Android version, language and app version;
  • how you interact with ads, such as which ads were shown and tapped;
  • diagnostic and performance information about the ad software.

This information is encrypted in transit. Google uses it under its own policies. See How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services and the Google Privacy Policy.

We don’t receive your personal information from Google. AdMob gives us only totals, such as how many ads were shown and what they earned. The game asks Google for ads suitable for teens.

Your ad choices

  • Consent (EEA, UK and Switzerland). When you first open the game, Google’s consent form asks whether ads may be personalised. You can change your choice at any time in Settings → Data → Ad privacy → Manage, which appears where the law requires it. Your choice is stored on your phone. Without consent you may still see ads that aren’t personalised; Google may still use limited data for them, for example to limit how often an ad is shown, to measure ads and to prevent fraud.
  • Any Android phone. In Android Settings → Privacy → Ads (the names vary by phone), you can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalised ads.
  • Data Google holds. You can review and manage it at myaccount.google.com.

Usage data

Settings → Data has a Share anonymous usage data switch. The current version of Peril Point doesn’t collect or send any usage statistics or crash reports, whichever way the switch is set. If that changes, we will update this policy first, saying what is collected and who processes it, and the switch will turn it off.

Deleting your data

  • Everything on your phone: clear the app’s storage in Android settings (Apps → Peril Point → Storage → Clear storage), or uninstall the game. Either erases everything Peril Point has stored, including your ad consent choice.
  • Only your progress: use Settings → Reset progress in the game. Your settings stay.
  • Ad data: reset or delete your advertising ID as described above, and manage data Google holds in your Google account.

Depending on where you live, for example under the Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012 or the EU and UK GDPR, you may have the right to access, correct, delete or object to the use of your personal information. We don’t hold personal information about you, so we have nothing to give or delete. For ad data, use the choices above or contact Google. You can still write to us with any question or complaint, and you may complain to your local data protection authority.

Children

Peril Point isn’t directed at children under 13, and we don’t knowingly allow them to use it. If you believe a child under 13 is using the game, you can delete its data as described above, or contact us.

Changes

If the game starts collecting anything new, such as usage statistics or crash reports, or uses a new ad or service provider, we will update this policy before that version is released and give it a new effective date.
